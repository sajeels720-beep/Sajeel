# Voiceover lines and file names

Record one audio file per line and name it as shown (`.wav` is best; `.mp3` or `.m4a` also work). Put them all in one folder, for example `voiceover/`. Each file is placed at its time slot in the edit and plays over the shots listed.

| File | Time slot (s) | Line | Plays over |
|---|---|---|---|
| `voiceover_01` | 0.0–3.0 | You take a giant bite of ice cream, | Shots 1–2 (spoon carves the scoop, POV bite) |
| `voiceover_02` | 3.0–5.2 | and for a second, it's perfect. | Shot 3 (blissful on the bench) |
| `voiceover_03` | 5.6–8.4 | Then a stabbing pain shoots through your forehead, | Shots 4–5 (crash zoom, ice cracks) |
| `voiceover_04` | 8.4–10.6 | like your brain is freezing solid. | Shot 6 (brain freezes over) |
| `voiceover_05` | 11.0–14.8 | You see, when something freezing hits the roof of your mouth, | Shots 7–8 (x-ray head, frost on the palate) |
| `voiceover_06` | 14.8–18.8 | the blood vessels in your head squeeze tight to hold in heat, | Shots 9–10 (vessels appear, then squeeze) |
| `voiceover_07` | 18.8–22.0 | then suddenly rush wide open to warm back up, | Shots 11–12 (vessels burst open, warm melt) |
| `voiceover_08` | 22.0–25.4 | and that flood of blood sets off pain sensors. | Shots 13–14 (blood rush, pain sparks) |
| `voiceover_09` | 25.9–29.6 | But the nerve carrying that signal also runs to your forehead, | Shots 15–16 (signal on the nerve, forehead branch) |
| `voiceover_10` | 29.6–32.0 | so that's where your brain feels it, | Shot 17 (red "?" and arrow) |
| `voiceover_11` | 32.0–36.8 | leaving you with a frozen brain that isn't frozen at all. | Shots 18–19b (frozen brain, ice shatters, another bite) |

The short gaps (5.2–5.6, 10.6–11.0 and 25.4–25.9 s) are deliberate beats before "Then", "You see" and "But".

Tips:
- Speak at a brisk, even pace like his (about 2.6 words per second). If a line comes out longer or shorter than its slot, that's fine: the edit is adjusted to your timing.
- A little silence at the start or end of a file is fine; it gets trimmed.
- Recorded everything in one take? Name it `voiceover_full` and leave a short pause between lines, so it can be split.
