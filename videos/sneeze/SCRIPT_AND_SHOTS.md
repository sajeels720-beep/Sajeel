# "What Happens If You Hold In A Sneeze 🤧" — script and shot plan

A 37-second Short (1,110 frames at 30 fps) in the same format as "Why Ice Cream Freezes Your Brain": second person, hook → **"You see,"** explanation → **"But…"** twist → callback to the opening.

## The new character

Not the brain-freeze guy. A young woman in her early twenties, built with MPFB / MakeHuman (all CC0):

- warm medium-brown skin, dark brown eyes, thick dark eyebrows;
- dark brown hair in a high **ponytail** (it swings on the sneeze, which gives good follow-through);
- a coral-orange T-shirt with **Jay3Dfilms** in white on the chest (warm orange pops against the blue backdrop, and it's a different colour from video 1's yellow), blue jeans;
- a big, rubbery face for the acting: nose twitch, "ahh… ahh…" squint, puffed cheeks, bulging eyes, the final sneeze.

## Backdrop

His studio blue, like your coin-toss screenshot: teal-cyan at the edges of the frame, fading smoothly to a pale blue-grey glow right behind the subject, a little deeper along the bottom. There's no horizon or floor line, and it looks the same from every camera angle. The colours were measured from your screenshot. The key light comes from the top right, the fill is tinted with the backdrop's blue, and a warm rim light separates her from it.

Inside-the-body close-ups (airway walls, eardrum, throat) use the warm tissue backdrop from video 1 instead, so anatomy never stands in front of the blue backdrop.

## Script (timed)

| Time (s) | Voiceover |
|---|---|
| 0.0–2.2 | You feel a giant sneeze coming, |
| 2.2–6.0 | so you pinch your nose, shut your mouth, and hold it in. |
| 6.3–7.5 | Big mistake. |
| 7.9–11.8 | **You see,** a sneeze blasts air out of your lungs super fast. |
| 11.8–14.6 | With nowhere to go, it gets trapped inside you, |
| 14.6–18.2 | and the pressure in your airways jumps about twenty times higher. |
| 18.2–21.2 | That air shoots up the tiny tubes to your ears, |
| 21.2–23.0 | and can burst your eardrums, |
| 23.0–25.6 | or pop the blood vessels in your eyes. |
| 26.0–30.0 | **But** in rare cases, it can even tear a hole in your throat. |
| 30.0–32.2 | So next time you feel one coming, |
| 32.2–33.6 | just let it out. |
| 33.6–35.2 | *(the sneeze: "Ahh… CHOO!")* |
| 35.2–37.0 | *(no voice: seeds float away, music button)* |

99 words, about 2.7 words per second, like video 1.

**Science check:**
- Doctors at the University of Dundee (BMJ Case Reports, 2023) reported a man who tore a 2 mm hole in his windpipe by holding in a sneeze with his nose pinched and mouth shut. They said this raises the pressure in the upper airways about 20 times.
- A 2018 case from Leicester (BMJ Case Reports) described a man who ruptured the back of his throat the same way.
- Burst eardrums (air forced up the Eustachian tubes) and burst blood vessels in the eyes are the other injuries doctors warn about.

All of these are rare, which is why the script says "can" and "in rare cases".

**Callback:** the video opens on the full seed head that starts the sneeze and ends on the same dandelion, blown bald by the sneeze.

**Workload:** 14 of the 22 shots are anatomy, props and effects (easier). Eight shots have her acting: 2–5 and 19–22. Shots 3, 20 and 21 need hand poses (pinching the nose, letting go), which are the hardest part, so those shots are kept short and close.

## Production checklist

- **Format:** 9:16, 1080×1920, 30 fps, 37.0 s = 1,110 frames.
- **Look:** AgX Punchy, studio blue backdrop with a pale glow behind the subject, shallow depth of field, motion blur, glare on glowing effects, slight chromatic aberration and grain.
- **Render:** EEVEE, 64 samples, one PNG sequence per shot, then the MP4.
- **Afterwards (you):** the voiceover (`VOICEOVER.md`) and sound effects. Then I build the final edit to your recording with captions and the mix.
